## Soccer Cleats by the Three Big Silos

Nike, adidas, and Puma run the soccer cleat world. Nike splits its roster three ways: the Mercurial for speed (Vapor 16 and Superfly 10, what Mbappe and Vinicius wear), the Tiempo for the leather-touch player (Legend 10 and the new Maestro the Germany squad started wearing in early 2026), and the Phantom for the precision passer (Phantom 6, what Bellingham wears for England). Adidas keeps three too: the Predator for control (Predator 26, Bellingham at Real Madrid), the F50 for speed (Messi for Inter Miami and Argentina, current Hora Dorada Pack), and the Copa Pure for the first-touch player (Copa Pure IV plus the Copa Mundial still in catalog forty-six years on). Puma runs the King for comfort (King 20), the Future for dribbling control (Future 9), and the Ultra for outright pace (Ultra 6). Below the three giants: Mizuno Morelia II out of Japan, New Balance Furon and Tekela, Joma and Charly across Liga MX, plus Sokito for the players choosing plant-based recycled uppers.

## Picking Your Plate: Firm Ground, Turf, Indoor, and the Rest

The plate underneath the soccer cleat has to match the surface. Firm Ground (FG) is for natural grass on a maintained pitch, what most Saturday rec leagues play on; the firm ground soccer cleats lineup runs deepest of any plate. Artificial Grass (AG) is for the green-plastic third-generation turf with shorter studs in a different pattern, won't tear up the surface or your knees. Soft Ground (SG) is for wet natural grass with removable metal studs, mostly a European pro thing. Multi-Ground (MG) splits FG and AG and works on both. Turf (TF) soccer shoes carry dozens of tiny rubber studs across the outsole. Indoor soccer shoes are flat-soled, for futsal courts and gym floors. The wrong plate on the wrong surface is the fastest way to roll an ankle.

## Picking by Position and by Player

Strikers and wingers lean speed: Mercurial, F50, Ultra, New Balance Furon. Lightweight, knit uppers, built around acceleration. Attacking midfielders split between control and touch: Phantom or Predator if your game is the through ball, Tiempo or Copa or King if your game is the first touch. Defensive midfielders and center-backs live in the control silos. Outside backs lean speed. Goalkeepers wear silo-specific keeper editions with grip patterns built for distribution.

## The Tier Ladder and the Kids Cut

Every silo runs a price ladder. Elite is the pro spec, K-leather or HybridTouch upper, $250 to $300. Pro sits just below at $180 to $220. League is where most club players actually buy: synthetic upper, 80 percent of the feel for half the cost, $80 to $120. Club is the entry tier, $50 to $80. Kids soccer cleats run the same silo names in junior cuts: Junior F50, Kids Predator Club, Junior Mercurial Academy. The silo defines the game; the tier defines the budget. The right silo at the League tier beats the wrong silo at the Elite tier every time.
